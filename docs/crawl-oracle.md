# The crawl oracle

The crawl oracle proves the engine's ported formulas against real crawl, so the engine's numbers can be trusted for each shipped Game Knowledge bundle. It is seam 2 in `CODING_STANDARDS.md`. It covers:

- a monster's melee attack on the player, unarmed or with melee weapons in its hands: the weapon the attack is made with, the monster's to-hit, which the weapon's accuracy and enchantment, the player's Lighting and the monster's Nightvision change, the roll against the player's evasion, the shield block, damage after armour with the weapon's damage and enchantment, what its brand adds for the player's resistances, holiness and god, and the delay its brand gives a melee action;
- a monster's spell cast at the player: which spell it picks, at what distance, the power and range it casts with, and the beam's accuracy, the shield block, the roll against evasion and damage after armour and the player's resistances, holiness and god;
- the gear crawl makes monsters with: the launcher or throwing missile, and the wand, each kind is given, against the odds in Game Knowledge;
- a monster's missile at the player: whether it shoots or throws from a distance and the energy that takes, with a heavy or fast launcher's delay, and the missile's to-hit and damage with a launcher's enchantment, the roll against evasion, the shield block, damage after armour and what a launcher's brand adds for the player's resistances, holiness and god;
- a monster's wand: whether it zaps it and the energy that takes, and the beam at the wand's power, with the player's resistances, holiness and god;
- who acts before the player's next standard-speed action: a monster's energy, what its walking, fighting and casting cost, how far its melee reaches and with which attacks, unarmed and holding a polearm, with a dagger in a second hand, how long a breath takes to catch, a monster that never moves or that steps toward the player only from beside them or by chance while they watch it, and whether the Threat Level counts it at all;
- the shield's block limit: how many attacks it blocks before the player's next action.

Spell failure, which only the player has, comes with the first player spell the engine ports (#35).

## How it works

### Melee

`tools/crawl-oracle/harness/melee.cc` is compiled against a crawl build's objects in place of `main.o`, as crawl's own `util/monster` is. It sets crawl up without a dungeon, then for each case:

- makes the player a human, a mummy, a demonspawn or a gargoyle, one holiness each that the engine tells apart, and dresses them at random (Strength, Dexterity, Dodging and Shields skill, body armour and a shield with random enchantment) until their AC, EV and SH are exact on the display, since the display is all the engine sees;
- places a random monster next to them with no statuses and, in every other case, no items. In the others it is of a kind that uses its starting equipment, and crawl's `give_specific_item` gives it a random melee weapon in its main hand, with an enchantment from -3 to +9 and a brand drawn as the missile harness draws a launcher's (below), and when it wields two weapons, half the time another in its off hand; it must make one of its damaging attacks with a weapon (`monster::weapon`);
- gives the player a god, none or a good god (Zin, the Shining One or Elyvilon) unless the light below made them a follower of Yredelemnul, and each of `rFire`, `rCold`, `rNeg`, `rPois`, `rElec` and `rCorr` at a random level from vulnerable to as resistant as it goes, by mutations and, for `rPois-`, poison vulnerability; it prints them as the character overview shows them (`_get_overview_resistances`), with the species and god, which is all the engine reads, and checks the player's AC, EV and SH haven't changed;
- picks one of the damaging attacks its kind defines, and runs it many times through crawl's own `melee_attack`, step by step, as `melee_attack::attack` does: `calc_to_hit`, `test_hit` against `player::evasion`, `attack_shield_blocked`, `calc_damage`, which applies armour, and `apply_damage_brand` on what the hit dealt, with the player's afflictions switched off as in the missile harness. It skips the other effects of a hit, and resets the shield's blocks and the player's hit points before each attack. Each time it also rolls the delay of a melee action by the monster (`monster::attack_delay`).

It prints a JSON line per case with the inputs the ported formulas take, the attack's slot and the weapons in each hand among them, and the tallies: how often each to-hit came up, hits, blocks, how often each amount of damage was taken, and how often each delay came up. The harness draws a weapon's kind from every melee weapon crawl defines, retired kinds such as the old hammer among them, but not the retired blowgun, which Game Knowledge doesn't count as a melee weapon.

It lights the player at random: a third of the time under a corona, which backlights them, a third as a follower of Yredelemnul, whose umbra surrounds them at any piety, and otherwise as the level and the monster leave them, so a monster with a halo or umbra still lights them. Each case prints the Lighting crawl's `player::backlit(false)` and `player::umbra()` give, which the engine takes, and the monster's `monster::nightvision()`, which must match its Game Knowledge. In the player's own umbra, the monster is drawn with Nightvision as often as without, as few kinds have it.

It leaves out what the engine doesn't model: animated objects, and a hydra's heads past its kind's attacks, which repeat its first. It also leaves out players whose AC, EV or SH carries a fraction the display drops: the oracle hands the engine their defences exactly, while the Threat Level, which sees only the display, guesses the fraction for each trial (#77). [The ledger](not-modelled.md) says why each matters and what modelling it would take.

### Spells

`tools/crawl-oracle/harness/spells.cc` is built the same way, but includes crawl's `mon-cast.cc`, whose spell choice is static, and links without `mon-cast.o`. It draws its cases from the spells the engine ports (`crawl-oracle ported-spells <crawl version>` lists them), alternating two kinds:

- **beam**: a randomly dressed player, lit at random as in the melee harness, and a random monster whose book holds a ported spell, casting it many times. Each cast is set up by crawl's `setup_mons_cast`, given the caster's Nightvision as `bolt::initialise_fire` gives it (`precalc_agent_properties`), and rolled as `bolt::affect_player` rolls it: `bolt::misses_player` for the shield and evasion, then the damage dice, `bolt::apply_AC` and `check_your_resists`, the player given a species, a god and resistances as in the melee harness. It prints the power, range and accuracy, and tallies hits, blocks and damage.
- **choice**: a random monster with a ported spell, a few squares from the player, picking a spell many times through crawl's `_choose_spell_to_cast`, from its book cut down to the ported spells (the engine assumes a monster doesn't cast the others). It tallies which spell, or none, was picked.

It leaves out what the engine doesn't model: draconians, whose breath their book doesn't hold; monsters whose spells crawl picks when they are made; and the beam's flight (`bolt::fire`), so a bounce or a chaos beam's flavour isn't diffed. A monster with an explosion or a cloud among its spells is kept three squares or more from the player in a choice case: up close, crawl's tracer weighs the harm the caster does itself against the harm to the player (`mons_should_fire`), which the engine doesn't port; it assumes the caster casts anyway. [The ledger](not-modelled.md) says why each matters and what modelling it would take.

### Missiles and wands

`tools/crawl-oracle/harness/missiles.cc` is built the same way, but includes crawl's `mon-act.cc`, whose `_handle_wand` is static, and links without `mon-act.o`. It runs crawl's console headless, as firing redraws the view. Five kinds of case take turns:

- **gear**: a random kind of monster that uses its starting equipment, three times in four one that a few tries show may be made with something to shoot or zap, is given its items by crawl's `give_item` many times, on D:5 in the Dungeon and not in a band, with the game's item sets picked afresh each time. It tallies the launcher, or else the throwing missile, it got, and the wand it got and will zap.
- **missile**: a monster made with a launcher or throwing missile fires it at a randomly dressed and randomly lit player many times, rolled as `ranged_attack::attack` rolls it: the to-hit, `attack::test_hit`, `attack_shield_blocked`, `calc_damage`, which applies armour, and `apply_damage_brand`, as `ranged_attack::handle_phase_hit` adds what the brand deals, without the other effects of a hit. A launcher is given an enchantment from -3 to +9 and a brand: none, each the engine ports (flaming, freezing, electrocution, holy wrath, foul flame, draining, pain, heavy and speed), or venom, which deals no damage of its own. A holy monster isn't given foul flame, an undead or demonic one holy wrath, nor a follower of Trog pain, which crawl gives them for nothing. The player is given a species, a god and resistances as in the melee harness. A thrown missile is unenchanted and unbranded. The player's afflictions are switched off (`DIS_AFFLICTIONS`), so draining doesn't take their maximum hit points away.
- **fire**: such a monster, its launcher wielded, one to seven squares from the player, decides many times whether to shoot or throw through crawl's `handle_throw`, which fires for real at a player whose hit points and statuses are restored each time. It tallies the energy each action took, 0 when it didn't fire. Nets are left out: one that hits catches the player.
- **wand**: a random monster zaps a wand of a spell the engine ports at a randomly dressed and randomly lit player many times, set up by `setup_mons_cast` as a wand zap and rolled as a spell's beam is, the player given a species, a god and resistances as in a missile case.
- **zap**: a monster given such a wand, which it likes, decides many times whether to zap it through crawl's `_handle_wand`, which zaps for real. It tallies the energy each action took. A wand of an explosion is zapped from three squares or more, as in the spell harness.

It leaves out what the engine doesn't model: a thrown missile's brand, a monster with both a launcher and missiles to throw, which takes its launcher two times in three, a monster swapping its launcher in from its off hand, which costs it a fifth of an attack's energy (`monster::swap_weapons`), the flight of a missile or beam past the player, and a monster weighing the harm a blast does itself. [The ledger](not-modelled.md) says why each matters and what modelling it would take.

### Turns

`tools/crawl-oracle/harness/turns.cc` is built the same way, but includes crawl's `mon-act.cc`, counting what a monster does as `mon-act.cc` calls into the code that does it, and links without `mon-act.o`. It runs crawl's console headless, as attacks redraw the view. It prints three kinds of case:

- **block**: for no shield, a buckler, a kite shield and a tower shield, how many attacks crawl's `actor::shield_block_limit` lets it block before the player's next action.
- **reach**: for every kind of monster with a damaging attack, made with no items, how far its melee reaches (`monster::reach_range`) and, from each distance up to that, which of its attacks it makes: those `melee_attack::can_reach` lets it make, but an attack only a weapon makes, which it doesn't make without one (`attack::init_attack`). A kind that uses its starting equipment and hits has a second reach case, holding a +0 spear, a polearm, in its main hand, and when it wields two weapons a +0 dagger in its off hand.
- **turn**: a random monster one to seven squares from the player, awake, hunting them, with no items, no statuses and its book cut down to the spells the engine ports, plays out the player's next standard-speed action many times, the player lit at random as in the melee harness. Each time it starts with the energy crawl leaves a monster that has acted (`monster::drain_action_energy`), with its hit points and its hit dice, which a spore attack spends, the player's hit points, statuses, mutations and banes are restored, a corona lit again, and the floor around them laid again, and crawl's own `handle_monsters` runs its turns with `you.time_taken` at `BASELINE_DELAY`, fighting and casting for real. It tallies the melee actions it took against the player, the spells it cast and the squares it closed. Turn cases take turns at drawing from kinds of monster that each exercise a rule: the slowest and the fastest that act (speeds 5 and 25 among those it keeps in 0.34.1), any kind, one faster or slower than the player, one that never moves, one that reaches, one that breathes, one that casts a blast or cloud, one that casts a blinkbolt, and, watched by the player, one that steps toward them only from beside them, the wandering mushroom or the deathcap, and one that steps only by chance, the lurking horror (`_fungal_move_check`). No spell the engine ports is instant in any 0.34.1 book, so the rule for instant spells has no kind to draw and is left out until one is.

It leaves out what the engine doesn't model, each by the `NotModelled` variant the Fact Sheet names it with: monster special abilities (`mon_special_ability`, `specialAbilities`), which it switches off, and the seekers, which burst by theirs; monsters that swoop or flank (`swoopOrFlank`), are batty (`batty`), keep their range (`keepsRange`), are tentacles or vines (`movesWithParent`), can't leave lava or water (`boundToTerrain`), or that `handle_monster_move` acts or moves for with code of its own, such as a hellfire mortar or an orb of destruction (`ownActions`); a monster faster than the player that casts or breathes a cloud, which crawl seldom casts again through the clouds it left before the player's next action (#104); draconians and books crawl fills when a monster is made, as in the spell harness; a shapeshifter, which takes another kind's shape as it is made; a mutant beast, whose facets, given as it is made, may add attacks its kind lacks (#95); and, found as a case runs, left out of that case and said so on standard error, any kind that dies in its own turns, moves the player, as a trampling or dragging hit does (`movesThePlayer`) or one that blinks with them (`blinksWithThePlayer`), or ends farther from them, as one whose hit blinks it away does (`blinksAway`). A caster of a blinkbolt, which carries it to the farthest square of the bolt's path it can stand on (`bolt::affect_endpoint`), past the player on open floor, has the square behind the player walled, which stops the bolt without its bouncing, so crawl lands it beside them as the engine does; a case that lands it anywhere else is left out and said so. A caster of an explosion or a cloud starts three squares or more away, as in the spell harness, and so does a kind that blows itself up as it attacks (`mons_self_destructs`), the lurking horror, which from there can't reach the player and attack before their next action, and so isn't left out for dying in its own turns. A cloud cast from a square past its range lands beside the player without its beam striking them, so only a monster crawl shows fighting the player, or one the engine's own trial strikes them with, must be one `threat::can_attack` counts. The harness can't tell whether a spell crawl cast struck the player, as some damage lands an action late, so a monster that only casts is checked by its casts' tally and the engine's own reach. [The ledger](not-modelled.md) says why each matters and what modelling it would take.

### Seeds and shards

Every harness takes `<seed> <cases> <trials>`, the file of ported spells if it draws from them, and optionally a shard, `<index>/<count>`. Each drawn case starts on a fresh level, with the player as crawl was set up, and crawl's RNG seeded from the run's seed and the case's index alone, so a case is drawn the same whatever came before it. With a shard, a harness draws only that shard's cases: `<count>` harnesses, one per shard, draw a run's cases between them, and their dumps together hold exactly the case lines one unsharded run prints. A harness's cases take turns at kinds, such as beam and choice in the spell harness, and each kind's cases are dealt round the shards in turn, so every shard gets its share of every kind; dealing the cases themselves would hand a shard only the kinds whose turn lines up with it, and some kinds, such as the fastest monsters' turns, take far longer than others. The dump's header names its shard.

The turn harness's `<cases>` counts its turn cases, and deals them by their number among the turn cases, each rule's round the shards. Its block and reach cases aren't drawn: they are the same every run. Every shard plays them and finds the kinds turn cases draw from, from the run's seed, so the turn cases are numbered alike in each; only shard 0 prints the block and reach cases.

### The diff

`crawl-oracle` reads any harness's dump, runs each case as many times through `copilot_core::oracle::melee`, `oracle::spell`, `oracle::spell_choice`, `oracle::gear`, `oracle::missile`, `oracle::fire`, `oracle::wand`, `oracle::zap` or `oracle::turn`, or against `oracle::shield_block_limit` or `oracle::melee_reach`, using the formula variants named in the bundle for that crawl version, and compares each tally with a chi-squared test. What the formulas and Game Knowledge fix, a spell's or wand's power, range and accuracy, a shield's block limit, a monster's reach and the attacks it makes from each distance, and its Nightvision, must be equal. A tally more than five standard deviations past chance is a mismatch, and any mismatch fails the run. Both sides are seeded per case, so the verdict is the same every run and on any shard. When it was built, an off-by-one put into each of the four melee formulas in turn failed between 11 and 175 of the 500 cases. In the spell formulas, one put into the beam's accuracy, the evasion roll, the shield's block, damage, armour or a spell's power failed between 22 and 261 of 500; two were too small to show at 20,000 trials, a block roll one higher and the spell roll taking a slot's frequency in 200 as `>` rather than `>=`, each shifting a chance by half a point. In the missile harness, 1,500 cases over three seeds match. An off-by-one put into a missile's accuracy, its damage or an archer's bonus damage, the chance a launcher holds fire (one in six for one in five) or a thrown missile is thrown (one in four for one in three), the chance a wand is zapped (two in three given up for three in four), a wand's power, or the draw of a monster's gear failed between 6 and 106 of 500; the archer's bonus, which only archers roll, was the smallest. With its launchers given an enchantment and a brand, 1,500 cases over three seeds match, every brand the engine ports drawn in each. An off-by-one put into a launcher's accuracy failed 100 of 500, into the damage its enchantment adds 10, flaming and freezing's damage 11, draining's 3, foul flame's 2, the chance electrocution strikes (one in three for one in four) 2, a heavy launcher's damage (twice for nine fifths) 2, and a heavy or a fast launcher's delay 3 and 4. Pain is struck seldom enough that one more point of its damage didn't show, though leaving it out failed 6. In the turn harness, 1,500 turn cases over three seeds match. An off-by-one put into a monster's starting energy or the energy it gains failed 467 of the 1,020 cases, a walk costing one more 152, a spell 52 and a melee action 33, a stationary monster walking 56, a reaching flavour reaching three squares 20, a breath caught one action sooner 12, an attack reaching one square past its own 3, and a shield blocking one more its block case. Its first runs found two errors in the engine: a reaching attack is made from as far as the monster reaches, so a starflower's lash follows its rift three squares, and a monster catches its breath after each of its own actions, not each of the player's, so a fast one may breathe twice. With every other melee case's monster holding weapons, 200 cases match, five of them with a second weapon, two of those attacking with it. An off-by-one put into a wielded weapon's accuracy failed 94 of the 200, leaving its enchantment out of its damage 59, its brand 46, its brand's delay 20, and making odd attacks with the main hand's weapon 4. The first run found a weapon whose enchantment crawl doesn't add to its damage, the retired blowgun (`attack::get_weapon_plus`), which Game Knowledge leaves out of the melee weapons. With the player given a species, a god and resistances at random, an engine that left their resistances out failed 21 of 100 spell cases, 6 of 100 melee cases and 10 of 100 missile and wand cases, and one that took no player for undead or demonic 5 of the 100 melee cases. With the wandering mushroom, deathcap and lurking horror drawn again, 500 turn cases match, 90 of them those kinds; an engine that walked them up as any other monster failed 83 of the 90, all but those beside the player. Their first run found the harness leaving a monster's hit dice spent between plays, which a spore attack spends, until a wandering mushroom died of it.

`cargo test` checks melee to-hit, a wielded weapon's enchantment and brand and a heavy weapon's delay, the player's resistances, holiness and good god, beam damage, spell power, spell choice, missile damage, a launcher's enchantment and brand and a heavy launcher's delay, the gear a kind is made with, how often a launcher fires, a shield's block limit, a monster's reach and the attacks it makes from afar, with a polearm and a second weapon, and the melee actions, squares closed and Threat Level count of a monster's turns.

## Running it

```sh
git clone --depth 1 --branch 0.34.1 https://github.com/crawl/crawl /tmp/crawl
git -C /tmp/crawl submodule update --init --depth 1 \
  crawl-ref/source/contrib/lua crawl-ref/source/contrib/pcre \
  crawl-ref/source/contrib/sqlite crawl-ref/source/contrib/zlib
tools/crawl-oracle/oracle.sh /tmp/crawl
```

The checkout must be at the bundle's commit (`crawl.commit` in `crates/gk/bundles/<tag>.json`). Crawl builds as webtiles and needs crawl's Linux build dependencies (`crawl-ref/INSTALL.md`). The first build takes a few minutes; later runs rebuild only the harnesses.

To diff one shard of the cases, pass it after the checkout: `tools/crawl-oracle/oracle.sh /tmp/crawl 1/4`. Run shards from separate checkouts, or one after another: each builds the harnesses in the checkout it is given.

The `Crawl oracle` workflow runs this for each stable bundle whenever the engine, the bundles or the oracle change, as a matrix of shards, one job each, so its wall time is a full run's work divided by the shards. When a growing engine makes the jobs slow, add shards to the `shard` matrix, all with the same count. It caches the crawl build by commit and runner OS image, saving it before the diff runs so a failing diff doesn't rebuild crawl next time.

`cargo test -p crawl-oracle` diffs a small dump from each harness (`tools/crawl-oracle/tests/fixtures/`), so every engine change is checked against crawl even without a crawl build. Regenerate a dump, unsharded, whenever its harness, its output or the ported spells change:

```sh
/tmp/crawl/crawl-ref/source/crawl-oracle-melee 34 24 20000 > tools/crawl-oracle/tests/fixtures/melee-0.34.1.jsonl
cargo run -q -p crawl-oracle -- ported-spells 0.34.1 > /tmp/ported-spells
/tmp/crawl/crawl-ref/source/crawl-oracle-spells 34 24 20000 /tmp/ported-spells > tools/crawl-oracle/tests/fixtures/spells-0.34.1.jsonl
/tmp/crawl/crawl-ref/source/crawl-oracle-missiles 34 60 20000 /tmp/ported-spells > tools/crawl-oracle/tests/fixtures/missiles-0.34.1.jsonl
/tmp/crawl/crawl-ref/source/crawl-oracle-turns 34 27 20000 /tmp/ported-spells > tools/crawl-oracle/tests/fixtures/turns-0.34.1.jsonl
```

The turn harness prints its block and reach cases whatever the count, so its dump is larger than the others.

## Adding a bundle

Add its tag to the workflow's `bundle` matrix. If its crawl changed the melee, spell, missile, wand or monster turn code, port a new formula variant first, and if it changed how monsters are given their gear, its series' gear rules in `tools/gk-extract`; the oracle shows where.
